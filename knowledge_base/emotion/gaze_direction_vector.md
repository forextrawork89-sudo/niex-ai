# Human Gaze Intelligence Framework

This document is the authoritative gaze foundation for the emotion subsystem.
It presents an encyclopedia-level framework for understanding human gaze as a layered phenomenon shaped by attention, intention, emotion, social norms, cognition, context, time, and bodily coordination.
The framework is conceptual and implementation-neutral. It avoids runtime architecture, memory allocation, browser execution details, worker architecture, WebAssembly references, and optimization details.
Its purpose is to explain how human gaze emerges, how it reflects attention and intention, how it interacts with emotion, how it evolves over time, and how it should be interpreted in multimodal systems.

Primary commitments:
* Gaze is understood as a behavioral, cognitive, social, and temporal signal rather than a single geometric feature.
* Human gaze reflects attention selection, social orientation, curiosity, intention, uncertainty, and emotional stance.
* Gaze analysis supports explainable reasoning instead of reductive or sexualized attention interpretation.
* The framework preserves uncertainty, context, culture, and temporal continuity.

## 1. Human Gaze Foundations

Human gaze emerges from the coordination of visual attention, head orientation, eye movement, cognitive goals, social expectations, and environmental monitoring.
Gaze is not merely a directional vector. It is a window into where a person is allocating attention, what they are trying to understand, and how they are relating to the world and to other people.
The foundations of gaze intelligence lie in recognizing that gaze can signal curiosity, concentration, hesitation, rapport, threat awareness, social alignment, and disengagement.

### 1.1 Attention Theory

Attention theory studies how people select, sustain, redirect, and disengage from information in the environment.
Gaze is one of the most observable outward markers of attention because eye direction often reflects where cognitive processing is currently directed.
Attention theory is crucial for explaining why gaze can be used to infer intention, uncertainty, learning engagement, and social orientation.

### 1.2 Visual Attention

Visual attention describes the selection of particular visual targets for detailed processing.
It explains why a person may look directly at an object, scan a space, hold fixation, or shift rapidly between multiple sources.
Visual attention is central to understanding how gaze reveals where cognitive resources are being deployed.

* 1.2.1: A case showing how visual attention is expressed through observed gaze patterns.
* 1.2.2: A case showing how visual attention is expressed through observed gaze patterns.

### 1.3 Social Attention

Social attention refers to the allocation of gaze to people, faces, gestures, and interactional cues.
Humans naturally monitor other people’s eyes, faces, mouths, and body orientation to interpret intent, emotion, and social stance.
Social attention helps explain why gaze is so important in conversation, teaching, caregiving, teamwork, and public speaking.

* 1.3.1: A case showing how social attention manifests through gaze toward faces, bodies, and interaction partners.

### 1.4 Gaze Behavior

Gaze behavior includes fixation, saccades, scanning, blinking, glancing, deliberate aversion, mutual gaze, and gaze withdrawal.
Gaze behavior is meaningful because it reveals underlying states such as interest, hesitation, discomfort, confidence, curiosity, control, and social alignment.
Gaze behavior is not only about the endpoint of the eye movement but also about how the movement unfolds over time.

* 1.4.1: A case showing how gaze behavior encodes engagement, uncertainty, and social orientation.

### 1.5 Cognitive Attention

Cognitive attention describes the intentional allocation of processing resources to a problem, stimulus, or task.
A student looking at a board, a teacher scanning the room, a surgeon monitoring a monitor, and an athlete tracking a ball all illustrate different forms of cognitive attention expressed through gaze.
Cognitive attention supports understanding why gaze can reflect learning states, concentration, confusion, anticipation, or distraction.

* 1.5.1: A case showing how cognitive attention appears in gaze behavior during problem solving and observation.

### 1.6 Environmental Monitoring

Environmental monitoring involves scanning the surroundings to assess risk, opportunity, movement, changes, and navigational constraints.
The gaze often tracks the environment to confirm safety, locate a target, anticipate motion, or coordinate action.
Environmental monitoring is important because it explains why gaze is not solely social but also ecological and action-oriented.

## 📐 Formulalar va metrikalar (v1 KB'dan)

The tracking of gaze trajectories relies on modeling the eye as a three-dimensional sphere within Euclidean space and computing an exit ray vector intersecting with a normalized two-dimensional screen plane ($P_{\text{screen}}$).

```text
             [Center of Pupil (C_pupil)]
                       \
                        v  .---.
                          /     \
                         |   .   | ----> [Gaze Vector Ray: R(t) = O + t * V_gaze]
                          \     /
                           '---'
                             |
                             v
    +-----------------------------------------------+
    |                                               |
    |      Screen Surface Plane (P_screen)          |
    |                                               |
    |            * [Gaze Intersection (X, Y)]       |
    |                                               |
    +-----------------------------------------------+
```

Let the origin of the ocular coordinate system (the center of the eye pupil) be defined as $O = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$. The directional tracking vector extracted via infrared pupil-glint reflections or neural facial landmark mesh points is denoted as:

$$\vec{V}_{\text{gaze}} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ v_y \\ v_z \end{bmatrix}$$

The parametric line representing the gaze ray $R(t)$ extending outward into space is formulated as:
$$R(t) = O + t \cdot \vec{V}_{\text{gaze}}, \quad \text{for } t \ge 0$$

To find the exact target intersection on the display surface plane, defined by the plane equation $\vec{N} \cdot (X - X_0) = 0$, the system computes the precise scalar parameter $t_{\text{hit}}$:

$$t_{\text{hit}} = \frac{\vec{N} \cdot (X_0 - O)}{\vec{N} \cdot \vec{V}_{\text{gaze}}}$$

The resultant coordinate mapping $X_{\text{target}} = R(t_{\text{hit}})$ yields the instantaneous $(x, y)$ pixel coordinates on the viewing canvas.

### 2.2 Stabilization and Temporal Dispersion Analytics
To distinguish normal exploratory biological eye movements from continuous concentrated focus, the module evaluates historical tracking over a rolling window of $k$ historical samples (where $k$ corresponds to $300\text{ ms}$ of capture latency).

The Gaze History Matrix ($V_{\text{gaze\_history}}$) is collected as:
$$V_{\text{gaze\_history}} = \{X_1, X_2, \dots, X_k\}$$

The physical stability index ($H_{\text{stability}}$) is calculated using the standard deviation of spatial coordinates over this historical window:

$$H_{\text{stability}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} (X_i - \mu_X)^2}$$

Where $\mu_X$ represents the mean geographic center of the gaze cluster during the specified interval.

* **Algorithmic Criteria:** High acceleration velocity spikes ($a_{\text{gaze}} > 15000\text{ px/s}^2$) accompanied by extreme dispersion metrics ($H_{\text{stability}} > 200\text{ px}$).

* **Algorithmic Criteria:** $15\text{ px} < H_{\text{stability}} \le 30\text{ px}$, but restricted to a highly limited surface layout while surrounding peripheral assets are fluctuating rapidly.
* **Neurological Context:** The user is hyper-focused on a singular task to the detriment of general situational awareness. Highly common in professional environments (e.g., flight operations, cyber defense operations) during high-stress alerts.
* **System Automation Reaction:** Triggers peripheral audio alerts to break the perceptual tunnel if external priority alarms are active.

#### State 05: Catatonic Drift / Micro-Sleep Anomaly
* **Algorithmic Criteria:** $H_{\text{stability}} < 3\text{ px}$ combined with an extended downward vector drift or absolute flatline data streams lasting longer than $6000\text{ ms}$.
* **Neurological Context:** The subject is experiencing fatigue, micro-sleep episodes, or has physically walked away from the workstation while utilizing an artificial photographic face print to spoof the biometric camera sensor.
* **System Automation Reaction:** Dispatches an immediate high-frequency acoustic verification challenge or enforces automatic workstation locking procedures (`LOCK_SECURE_MODE`).

## 4. Hardware Calibration, Signal Anomalies, & Mitigations

Gaze tracking vectors are highly prone to environmental noise, hardware drifts, and anatomical variance. CAM-GDV maintains real-time filtering layers to counteract these errors:

### 4.1 Strabismus, Astigmatism, and Ocular Asymmetry
* **The Problem:** Subjects with lazy eyes (strabismus) or significant refractive differences produce dual gaze vectors ($\vec{V}_{\text{gaze\_left}}$ and $\vec{V}_{\text{gaze\_right}}$) that diverge by more than $15^\circ$, causing the system to calculate conflicting point intersections.
* **The Mitigation Engine:** The module tracks eye vector correlation metrics continuously. If divergence stays uniform over $5000\text{ ms}$, the system identifies the dominant tracking eye based on higher validation scores. The non-dominant eye data is omitted from vector aggregation loops to avoid tracking blur, assigning a `SYS_ASYMMETRIC_EYE_CALIBRATED` status flag.

### 4.2 Nystagmus and Pathological Tremor Separation
* **The Problem:** Involuntary rhythmic oscillation of the eyeballs (nystagmus) mimics high-frequency saccadic flight patterns, leading to false-positive classification of user panic or automation spoofing.
* **The Mitigation Engine:** Applies a dynamic low-pass Butterworth filter configured with a sharp cutoff frequency ($f_c = 15\text{ Hz}$). This filters out microscopic involuntary oscillations while retaining deliberate macropursuit tracking vectors.

### 4.3 Eyewear Glare and Reflections (Specular Distortion)
* **The Problem:** Standard prescription eyewear or blue-light blocking coatings reflect ambient light sources (monitors, overhead LEDs), creating false pupil glint vectors that corrupt center-of-mass pupil estimations.
* **The Mitigation Engine:** Activates a spatial contrast masking routine. If a glint artifact displays an unnatural shape or lacks a matching infrared return signature, the system masks the glare pixels and interpolates the remaining pupil boundary using an ellipse-fitting algorithm.
