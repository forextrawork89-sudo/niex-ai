# Multimodal safety context patterns

## Purpose
This file strengthens safety reasoning around clothing, pose, framing, movement, and scene context so that visual signals are treated as contextual evidence rather than standalone verdicts.

## Clothing context
- Ordinary clothing, sportswear, swimwear, or casual attire are not inherently harmful.
- Clothing becomes more relevant when paired with privacy violation, sexualized framing, target age, or coercive intent.
- A visible swimsuit, crop top, or fitted outfit can be contextual evidence only when combined with other signals.

## Pose and motion
- Ordinary dancing, posing, stretching, athletics, or fashion photography are not inherently harmful.
- Repeated suggestive motion, groping, object manipulation, or coercive framing can increase risk when combined with other signals.

## Framing and camera context
- Close-up framing, repeated focus on body parts, or cropped composition can increase sensitivity, but should not independently produce a block.
- Neutral or educational framing should reduce risk.

## Scene context
- Bedrooms, private interiors, hidden spaces, or secretive settings can contribute to risk only when coupled with other harmful signals.
- Public events, sports, dance classes, or family gatherings should not be treated as harmful solely because of location.

## Temporal video patterns
- Repeated suspicious motion, scene changes, or persistent focus on intimate body regions across frames may increase risk.
- A single suspicious frame should not trigger a block without supporting evidence.

## Safe-context exceptions
- Dance practice, sports, fashion, fitness, school events, or artistic performance are safe unless accompanied by explicit coercion, exploitation, or sexualized intent.
