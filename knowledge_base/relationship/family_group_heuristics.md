# Family Relationship Intelligence Framework

## 1. Purpose and governing philosophy

This file is a complete knowledge-base reconstruction for family-group reasoning in human scenes. Its purpose is to explain how family relationships are identified, how family interactions differ from generic multi-person proximity, and why a scene is likely to represent a legitimate family or caregiver relationship rather than a merely spatial cluster of people.

The framework treats family-group reasoning as a layered, explainable process based on interaction behavior, social context, developmental stage, caregiving signals, shared attention, temporal continuity, and environmental evidence. It does not reduce family interpretation to bounding-box sizes, body-height ratios, overlap percentages, proximity thresholds, or simple adult-child heuristics.

A family scene is recognized not simply because people are close to one another, but because the interaction pattern is coherent, persistent, and socially meaningful. A parent and child, siblings, grandparents and grandchildren, caregivers and patients, or relatives at a celebration all share evidence that supports a family interpretation across time and context.

## 2. Family relationship foundations

### 2.1 Family psychology
Family psychology studies the emotional, developmental, and relational dynamics that shape care, attachment, conflict, cooperation, and belonging. Family-group interpretation depends on recognizing repeated relational patterns such as caregiving, coordination, shared attention, comfort, teaching, protection, and social belonging.

### 2.2 Developmental psychology
Developmental psychology explains how children, adolescents, and adults differ in communication, dependency, autonomy, play, social learning, and emotional regulation. Family interpretation is richer when it accounts for age-related behavior rather than relying on size alone.

### 2.3 Caregiver-child interaction
Caregiver-child interaction includes holding, guiding, protecting, assisting, teaching, feeding, comforting, and supervising. These interactions carry strong evidence for family or caregiving bonds because they are not random social contacts.

### 2.4 Social relationships
Social relationships span friendship, acquaintance, teamwork, authority, mentorship, and kinship. Family interpretation depends on distinguishing kin-based patterns from other social interactions that may be visually similar.

### 2.5 Attachment behavior
Attachment behavior includes seeking proximity, following, staying near, checking back, seeking reassurance, and looking for comfort. These are meaningful signs of an emotionally organized relationship, particularly in parent-child or caregiver-child contexts.

### 2.6 Interpersonal behavior
Interpersonal behavior includes eye contact, turn-taking, coordinated action, conversation, and emotional responsiveness. Family scenes often display sustained interpersonal coordination that differs from casual crowding or temporary public contact.

### 2.7 Household activities
Household activities such as meals, cleanup, getting ready, reading, homework, bedtime, grooming, and resting provide social structure that supports family interpretation. The home environment amplifies the meaning of simple gestures.

### 2.8 Family routines
Family routines establish repeated patterns of responsibility, scheduling, coordination, caregiving, and emotional exchange. The presence of recurring behavior is highly informative because it suggests long-term relationship structure.

### 2.9 Social bonding
Social bonding includes closeness, reassurance, touch, shared attention, mutual familiarity, and emotional responsiveness. These patterns are essential for distinguishing family from incidental social proximity.

### 2.10 Group dynamics
Group dynamics studies how multiple people coordinate and influence one another. Family-group reasoning depends on whether the group behaves as an integrated social unit rather than as independent people sharing space.

### 2.11 Human relationship perception
Human relationship perception is the human tendency to infer kinship, caregiving, friendship, and authority from behavior. In explainable AI systems, this perception must be reconstructed in a structured and evidence-based way rather than assumed from appearance alone.

### 2.12 Family scene understanding
Family scene understanding requires integrating the participants, their interactions, their age composition, the environment, the activity, and the social meaning of the moment. It is not enough to see several people near one another.

### 2.1 Scale Inversion Ratio ($R_{\text{scale}}$)

$$R_{\text{scale}} = \frac{\min(\text{Height}(B_{\text{subject}_A}), \text{Height}(B_{\text{subject}_B}))}{\max(\text{Height}(B_{\text{subject}_A}), \text{Height}(B_{\text{subject}_B}))}$$

A scale ratio where $R_{\text{scale}} \le 0.65$ combined with a localized surface intersection indicates a multi-generational (Adult-Child) pairing matrix layout.

### 2.2 Shared Centroid Group Proximity ($D_{\text{group}}$)
The overall group dispersion metric across $N$ co-present subjects is calculated using variance over normalized spatial centroids:

$$\mu_x = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_{\text{center}, i}, \quad \mu_y = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} y_{\text{center}, i}$$

$$D_{\text{group}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sqrt{(x_{\text{center}, i} - \mu_x)^2 + (y_{\text{center}, i} - \mu_y)^2}$$

        if (!detectedPeopleMatrix || totalPeopleCount < 2) {
            this.accumulatedFamilyScore = this.accumulatedFamilyScore * FAMILY_HEURISTICS_CONFIG.ALPHA_TEMPORAL_SMOOTHING;

        let cumulativeClosenessMetric = 0.0;
        let validInteractionPairs = 0;

            const confI = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideI];
            if (confI < FAMILY_HEURISTICS_CONFIG.MIN_BOX_CONFIDENCE) continue;

            const xMinI = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideI + 1];
            const yMinI = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideI + 2];
            const xMaxI = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideI + 3];
            const yMaxI = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideI + 4];
            const hI = yMaxI - yMinI;
            const cxI = xMinI + (xMaxI - xMinI) * 0.5;
            const cyI = yMinI + (hI) * 0.5;

            for (let j = i + 1; j < subjectsCount; j++) {
                const strideJ = j * 5;
                const confJ = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideJ];
                if (confJ < FAMILY_HEURISTICS_CONFIG.MIN_BOX_CONFIDENCE) continue;

                const xMinJ = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideJ + 1];
                const yMinJ = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideJ + 2];
                const xMaxJ = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideJ + 3];
                const yMaxJ = detectedPeopleMatrix[strideJ + 4];
                const hJ = yMaxJ - yMinJ;

                const cyJ = yMinJ + (hJ) * 0.5;

                // 3. Proximity and Centroid Separation Mechanics

                const dy = cyI - cyJ;
                const distanceBetweenCentroids = Math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy);

                validInteractionPairs++;

            frameInstantaneousScore = 0.90; // High probability of secure family grouping

            frameInstantaneousScore = 0.65; // Standard multi-person social crowd
        }

        this.accumulatedFamilyScore = (this.accumulatedFamilyScore * FAMILY_HEURISTICS_CONFIG.ALPHA_TEMPORAL_SMOOTHING) +

        return this._packageTelemetryPayload(isFamilySafeContextConfirmed, this.accumulatedFamilyScore, multiGenerationalPairingsCount);
