# Sports, Yoga & Exercise Context Intelligence Framework

This document is a research-grade specification for sports, yoga, and exercise activity reasoning as a foundational component of multimodal scene understanding and policy enforcement. It explains how body positions, movement patterns, biomechanics, equipment, instructional context, and environment combine to create explainable reasoning about legitimate athletic, therapeutic, and wellness activities.

Sports and yoga reasoning is not pose angle thresholds. Sports reasoning is not skeletal symmetry calculations or flexibility scores. Exercise reasoning is not pelvic position or simple discount multipliers. Sports and yoga reasoning is unified understanding of what activity is occurring, why it is occurring, what equipment and context support it, what biomechanical evidence validates it, and what policy implications it has.

Sports and exercise reasoning integrates pose understanding, movement analysis, temporal patterns, object recognition (equipment), environmental context, instructional evidence, behavioral signals, and domain-specific knowledge about athletic and therapeutic practices. Strong exercise reasoning is interpretable, explicitly documents uncertainty, preserves confidence architecture, and propagates evidence through hierarchical reasoning stages.

## 1. Foundational Principles of Sports and Exercise Understanding
Sports, exercise, yoga, and rehabilitation are legitimate human activities that involve structured movement, intentional training, skill development, health improvement, and therapeutic benefit.
Exercise reasoning is contextual. The same body position carries different meaning in yoga studio versus a non-exercise environment. Stretching in a warm-up context differs from static posing in other contexts. Athletic movement differs from casual movement.
Exercise is multimodal. Body configuration is visible. Movement patterns emerge across time. Equipment presence signals athletic or therapeutic intent. Environment type constrains what activities are reasonable. Instructions and coaching indicate structured learning.
Exercise is hierarchical. Low-level evidence about joint angles aggregates into movement patterns, which aggregate into exercise identification, which aggregates into training purpose, which aggregates into activity validation and policy reasoning.
Exercise is temporal. Single frames provide limited evidence. Video sequences show movement continuity, repetition, fatigue, progression, and training structure that validate exercise interpretation.
Exercise has biomechanics. Athletic and therapeutic movements obey biomechanical principles. Bodies move in characteristic ways during training. Movements show coordination and control expected from intentional exercise rather than incidental motion.
Exercise context validates interpretation. Gym equipment, yoga studio setting, exercise clothing, coach presence, training facility, instruction, and group participation all support exercise interpretation.
Exercise reasoning must be explainable. Every exercise interpretation must trace how evidence combines into understanding. Confidence must be justified by specific biomechanical and contextual evidence. Uncertainty must be explicit.
